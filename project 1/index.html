Federo

Federo is a Art Deco-inspired, neo-grotesque sans-serif display webfont that references Jakob Erbar's Feder Grotesk. The goal was to keep the typeface as close as possible to the original 1909 design while adapting it for crisp web typography. Details were refined and contrast was slightly reduced for consistency. Figures obtained regular proportions and counters were increased for better legibility. Federo was designed by Olexa Volochay in 2011. Feder-Grotesk, Federo’s predecessor was designed by Jakob Erbar and first cast by Ludwig & Mayer in 1909.

The Jazz age represented a cultural shift towards freedom and was a huge part of the 1920’s. It was played everywhere, clubs, dance halls, and parties, it became connected with the fun and new exciting lifestyle post-war. The music was also influencing design. Everything started to feel faster, bolder, and more energetic. The designs at this time had sharp angles, symmetry and repetition.

The 1920’s were all about looking stylish and living the lavish lifestyle. Fashion, jewelry & fancy parties, think The Great Gatsby. These things all helped shape the glamorous and luxurious look we still associate with the era today. Designers used decorative patterns, metallic surfaces and dramatic silhouettes to create an air of glamor and luxury within their designs.

The early 1900’s brought a lot of new technology and ideas. The invention of cars, radios, airplanes, movies and skyscrapers all made the world feel like it was moving faster than it ever had. Art Deco architecture was known for bold shapes, symmetry and decorative details. Buildings like skyscrapers used these things to create something that was both luxurious and futuristic.
